Medical Translator Website

This translates complex medical terms into simple language.

Complex Medical Term Simplified Meaning
Abrasion A scrape or minor cut on the surface of the skin
Acute Sudden and short-term (happening quickly)
Benign Not cancerous and usually not harmful
Chronic Long-lasting or ongoing (lasting for three months or longer)
Contusion A bruise caused by an injury under the skin
Cyanosis Bluish color in the skin, lips, or nails caused by low oxygen
Dyspnea Shortness of breath or trouble breathing
Edema Swelling caused by extra fluid trapped in the body
Emesis Vomiting or throwing up
Febrile Having a fever (high body temperature)
Fracture A broken or cracked bone
Hypertension High blood pressure
Hypotension Low blood plessure
Inflammation Redness, swelling, heat, or pain in part of the body
Malignant Cancerous and able to spread to other parts of the body
Syncope Fainting or briefly passing out
Arrhythmia An irregular heartbeat (beating too fast, too slow, or unevenly)
Cerebrovascular Accident (CVA) A stroke (blocked or burst blood vessel in the brain)
Hemorrhage Heavy or uncontrolled bleeding
Hypoxia Low oxygen levels in the body's tissues
Ischemia Reduced blood flow to a part of the body
Myocardial Infarction (MI) A heart attack (blocked blood flow to the heart muscle)
Pneumonia A lung infection that fills air sacs with fluid or pus
Tachycardia A heart rate that is faster than normal
Adverse Reaction An unexpected or harmful side effect from a medicine
Analgesic Pain reliever medicine
Antibiotic Medicine that fights infections caused by bacteria
Anticoagulant Blood thinner (medicine that helps prevent blood clots)
Antiemetic Medicine that stops nausea and vomiting
Antipyretic Fever-reducing medicine
Contraindication A reason why a patient should not take a certain medicine or treatment
Oral (PO) Taken by mouth (swallowed)
PRN (Pro Re Nata) Take only as needed (for example, when you have pain)
Subcutaneous Given as a shot just under the skin
Topical Put directly onto the skin (like a cream or ointment)
Biopsy Removing a tiny piece of tissue to check it under a microscope
Complete Blood Count (CBC) A standard blood test that checks red cells, white cells, and platelets
Electrocardiogram (ECG/EKG) A quick, painless test that checks the electrical activity of your heart
Incision A clean cut made into the skin during surgery
Intravenous (IV) Giving fluids or medicine directly into a vein through a small tube
Lesion Any abnormal spot, sore, lump, or damaged area on or inside the body
Pathology Lab testing of blood, body fluids, or tissue to find the cause of an illness
Suture Stiches used to close a cut or surgical wound
Ultrasound A painless scan that uses sound waves to take pictures inside the body
Ambulatory Able to walk around on your own
Aspiration Food, liquid, or saliva accidentally going down the windpipe into the lungs
Inpatient Staying overnight in the hospital for medical care
NPO (Nil Per Os) Nothing to eat or drink (not even water) before a test or surgery
Outpatient Getting medical care or a procedure without staying overnight in the hospital
Prognosis What doctors expect to happen next with an illness or recovery
